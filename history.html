Muster and organization, 1861

The 1st Ohio Volunteer Cavalry was organized in the summer and fall of 1861. Company A was accepted into United States service on 19 August 1861 at Camp Chase, near Columbus, Ohio, under the command of Captain John H. Robinson. The men signed on for three years.

Camp Chase sat on the western edge of Columbus. The men drilled there while the regiment assembled its full complement of ten companies. It was a place of heat, mud, bad food and green recruits learning to ride together — the standard beginning for any Civil War cavalry regiment.

Source: compiled muster-in card, Captain John H. Robinson's company.

Bloomery Furnace, February 1862

The moment that defined Company A's reputation came on 14 February 1862 at Bloomery Furnace, Virginia, in what is now West Virginia. Brigadier General Frederick W. Lander was driving Confederate forces from the region in midwinter. When the lead Union regiment faltered and held back from advancing, Lander lost patience.

The company history records what happened next. Lander turned to the men who had followed him to the front — among them Lieutenant Noah Jones and the troopers of Company A — and called out:

"If you men will not go forward, give way to the Ohio boys!"

And then the Ohio boys went forward. The motto has stayed with the unit ever since.

Source: Company A history, p. 50. Lander's exact words as recorded there. See also the unit motto documentation in the binder.

The Shenandoah Valley and the East, 1862

Company A spent much of 1862 in the Shenandoah Valley and northern Virginia, moving with the shifting fortunes of the Union armies in the Eastern Theater.

  1. 23 Mar 1862
    Kernstown, Virginia. The first major engagement in the Valley campaign. The company history names Companies A and C as present.Source: Company A history and letters home
  2. 30 May 1862
    Front Royal. Confederate forces under Stonewall Jackson struck hard at Front Royal. The regiment was engaged as the Union position in the Valley began to collapse.Source: official engagement record, Appendix E
  3. 8–9 Jun 1862
    Port Republic. The final engagement of Jackson's Valley Campaign. The regiment was present as Jackson defeated the Union forces under Shields and Frémont.Source: official engagement record, Appendix E
  4. 27 Jul 1862
    Company A was detached and assigned as escort to Major General John Pope, commanding the Army of Virginia. The company was sent from Alexandria to join Pope at Warrenton.Source: record-of-events card
  5. 22 Aug 1862
    Catlett's Station. J.E.B. Stuart's Confederate cavalry raided Pope's headquarters at Catlett's Station, capturing documents and causing significant disruption.Source: official engagement record, Appendix E
  6. 29–30 Aug 1862
    Second Bull Run. Pope's army was defeated by Lee and Jackson on the old Manassas battlefield. The regiment was engaged in the fighting and covering the Union retreat.Source: official engagement record, Appendix E

1863 and the question of Gettysburg

By 1863, Company A was serving in a headquarters escort and detached duty role in the Eastern Theater. The regiment's exact movements through the Gettysburg campaign are still being traced in the records.

Gettysburg, 1–3 July 1863. Where the company stood at Gettysburg is still disputed in our research. The regiment's presence at the battle is on the official record, but the company-level documentation has gaps. We follow the records where they lead and say so when they run out.

What is clear: the regiment was also at Monterey Pass on 4–5 July 1863, the pursuit action after Gettysburg as Lee's army retreated through the mountains back to Virginia, and at Mine Run in November–December 1863, the inconclusive campaign in central Virginia.

Source: Gettysburg — open question, still under research. Monterey Pass and Mine Run — official engagement record, Appendix E.

Going west, 1864–1865

In 1864 the regiment transferred from the Eastern Theater to operations in the western states. The company's records show stations at Nashville, Gravelly Springs (Alabama), Macon and Orangeburg.

  1. Feb–Mar 1864
    Kilpatrick's Raid on Richmond. The regiment participated in the daring cavalry raid aimed at liberating Union prisoners held at Belle Isle and Libby Prison in Richmond.Source: official engagement record, Appendix E
  2. May 1865
    The Pursuit of Jefferson Davis. As the Confederacy collapsed, the regiment was part of the forces that hunted down Confederate President Jefferson Davis following Lee's surrender. Davis was captured in Georgia on 10 May 1865.Source: official engagement record, Appendix E
  3. 13 Sep 1865
    Company A was honorably discharged at Hilton Head, South Carolina, nearly four years after mustering in at Camp Chase. The men who had enlisted for three years had served past the end of the war itself.Source: discharge certificate

What the records say

Our portrayal is built on the original documents, not the summaries. When a source says one thing and a secondary account says another, we go back to the primary. When we don't know, we say so.

The core records for Company A's service include the compiled muster-in and muster-out cards, the record-of-events cards, the company history (compiled after the war), letters home preserved in pension files, and the official engagement record in Appendix E of the regimental history.
